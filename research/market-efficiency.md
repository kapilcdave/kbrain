# Market Efficiency

## Summary

Across weather, sports, short-duration crypto, and decentralized exchanges,
quoted prices were difficult to improve after costs. Small statistical
deviations existed, but economic efficiency did not require perfect calibration.

## Findings

- **Measured:** Short-duration crypto prices were strongly calibrated out of
  sample.
- **Measured:** Pregame baseball prices showed no robust stale-line gradient and
  remained tightly quoted.
- **Measured:** Weather distributions were imperfect at listing, but the
  deviation was generally smaller than execution costs.
- **Measured:** Cross-market consistency tests found no violation or found
  opportunities too small and illiquid to execute.
- **Inference:** Tight, liquid markets tend to be well calibrated. Wide markets
  often lack usable flow rather than informed competition.
- **Conclusion:** A model should be judged by incremental value over the market
  price, not standalone predictive accuracy.

## Limits

Efficiency is conditional on the observed information set, horizon, venue, and
period. These findings do not imply every contract is efficient.
